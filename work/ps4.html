A

Brief and measurable targets

Historical wastage: steel 12%, plywood formwork 9% given. Excavation continues for 30 days given. In that time we set up control of BBS, ordering (project-wise or block-wise), storage and release to fabricators, and reconciliation, to bring steel wastage to 5%. A similar system for formwork must reduce wastage by 4%, group columns and beams by size, and reuse a formwork set at least 10 times.

B

Why steel and formwork are wasted

Design & BBS

  • Errors in cutting lengths
  • Old drawing revision used
  • Late design changes

Cutting

  • No cutting plan for 12 m bars
  • Short offcuts discarded
  • Cut-to-fit on site

Store

  • Over-ordering
  • Rust, mixed diameters
  • Theft; no issue records

Formwork

  • Nailing through the face
  • Early or rough stripping
  • No release agent; poor storage
  • Panels cut for one-off sizes

Not every difference is waste. Steel is bought by weighbridge weight but a BBS uses theoretical mass; bar mass tolerances and weighing practice create differences. These are investigated, not automatically booked as wastage.

C

BBS workflow and illustrative example

  1. SourceLatest approved Good For Construction (GFC) structural drawings; drawing number and revision written on every BBS sheet.
  2. PrepareBBS engineer lists member, location, bar mark, diameter, shape, cutting length, number, total length, unit mass and total mass. Laps, anchorage, hooks and bends from the drawings and the applicable detailing standard.
  3. CheckChecked and signed by the responsible engineer before any cutting.
  4. ReviseA revised drawing means a revised BBS before the next issue; old sheets withdrawn.
  5. UseThe approved BBS drives ordering (D), issue (E) and reconciliation (H).

Unit mass

m = d² / 162 kg/m (d in mm) — standard approximation
Exact: (π/4)·d²·7850 / 10⁶ → 20 mm: 2.466 vs 2.469 kg/m

Use one source and one rounding method throughout.

D

Ordering: project-wise or block-wise?

OptionAdvantageProblem
Project-wise bulk purchaseBest price; one negotiationLarge stock, rust and theft risk, cash locked up, more handling
Block-wise purchaseStock matches the next pours; easier reconciliationPrice risk and more negotiations if each order is separate
Proposed: hybridProject-wide rate contract + block / pour-wise call-off ordersNeeds a rolling 3-week look-ahead from planning

Rate contract (whole project)

  • Brand, grade and diameters
  • Price and price-variation basis
  • Delivery lead time
  • Mill test certificate and heat number per lot
  • Option for cut-and-bend supply

Call-off order (per block / pour)

  • Quantity from the approved BBS of the next pours plus a small handling allowance
  • Diameter-wise, in standard lengths
  • Delivered about a week before fabrication
E

Receiving, storage, issue and fabrication control

Receive→Weigh & check MTC→Diameter bay→Issue vs BBS→Cut & bend→Fix→Return offcuts
  • Receive: weighbridge slip vs invoice; mill test certificate and heat number checked; sample bar weighed per lot to record actual kg/m.
  • Store: by diameter, grade and heat/lot on raised supports; covered; tagged; protected from mud, oil and uncontrolled corrosion; first in, first out.
  • Issue: only against an issue slip naming the BBS sheet and bar marks — never a lump-sum quantity.
  • Fabricate: from the combined cutting list; every bundle tagged with its bar mark.
  • Return: offcuts sorted by diameter and length; scrap weighed before sale.

Offcut rules

  • Offcuts go to a rack by diameter and length band
  • A minimum practical reuse length is set by the BBS engineer
  • Reuse in designed members only where it matches a bar mark and the engineer approves
  • Not every offcut is reusable; short pieces are scrap, weighed and recorded

F

Reconciliation and the wastage KPI

Adopted weekly reconciliation (issued-quantity basis): Wastage % = (Steel issued to fabrication − Steel incorporated per approved BBS − Accepted reusable returns − Work-in-progress awaiting fixing) ÷ Steel issued × 100. Purchases are tracked separately as receipts and closing stock; do not treat unissued stock as wastage. Use the same issue-based definition and cut-off rules for both the 12% baseline and the 5% target. For a project-level purchase KPI, reconcile opening stock + receipts − closing stock before calculating consumption.

G

Formwork: quantity, grouping and reuse

Measuring formwork

Formwork is measured as the area of concrete in contact with the form, in m², following the contract's measurement rules. Example: a 450 × 450 column with 3.0 m clear height has 4 × 0.45 × 3.0 = 5.4 m² of contact area per lift example.

Reuse SOP for a ≥ 10-cycle target

Ten cycles is a target, not a property of plywood. Ordinary timber formwork is often reused only a few times; reaching 10 depends on the plywood grade and thickness, the system, workmanship and inspection.

  1. SelectFilm-faced shuttering plywood of suitable grade and thickness on battens or a system frame; cut edges sealed.
  2. IdentifyPanel ID and cycle counter on every panel.
  3. PrepareRelease agent before every use; correct bracing and props.
  4. Pour carefullyControlled placing and vibration so the face is not damaged.
  5. Strike on timeOnly after the approved striking time (IS 456 cl. 11.3 gives guidance for ordinary Portland cement, subject to the engineer's approval).
  6. Clean, inspect, repairScrape and clean; inspect after every cycle; repair edges and faces.
  7. StoreFlat, dry, off the ground, covered.

Data limitations & assumptions

  • No GFC structural drawings: member sizes C1, C2, B1, B2 are from preliminary notes; the BBS example's reinforcement is assumed.
  • 1,320 t steel uses an assumed 110 kg/m³; Rs 60/kg is an indicative Aug 2026 rate; scrap value not deducted.
  • Contact-area ratio (6 m²/m³), the 4-cycle baseline and the Rs 2,000 sheet rate are assumptions.
  • 'Reduce formwork wastage by 4%' is read as 9% → 5%; confirm with faculty.

Conclusion

Steel is controlled by a chain the site can run: approved BBS → combined cutting list → project rate contract with pour-wise call-offs → tagged storage → issue against bar marks → offcut rack → weekly reconciliation with a fixed wastage definition. Meeting the 5% target would save about 111 t on the planning estimate (purchased basis), worth roughly Rs 66 lakh at indicative rates. Formwork is standardised into member groups. A panel-wise SOP with inspection after every cycle gives the 10-cycle target a realistic chance; it is a target to be monitored, not a guaranteed result.
