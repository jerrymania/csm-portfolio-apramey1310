A

The brief

Using the concrete quantity for each block, find the number of skilled workers (fabricators, carpenters, masons) and unskilled workers (the concrete gang) through the stages of construction. There will be peaks and slack periods; too few workers cost productivity, too many leave because there is no work. We must also know prevailing and statutory minimum wages, and the legal and technical content of a labour-supply contract.

How the levelling was done

  1. Find the floatIn this illustrative logic Phases 1 and 2 are critical (early handover of those blocks). Phase 3's chain finishes blockwork in M22 against an M24 completion, so it has 2 months of total float.
  2. Shift only the non-critical chainPhase 3 (Blocks 4, 7) moved 2 months later. Phases 1 and 2 are not moved.
  3. RecalculateThe month-9 overlap of Phase 1 frame, Phase 2 frame and Phase 3 substructure disappears. Peak drops from 132 to 97; finish stays within M24.
  4. Note the costThe shift uses all of Phase 3's float, so Phase 3 becomes critical. A 1-month shift would keep a buffer but level less.

Under-hiring

  • Pours slip; formwork and steel gangs become the bottleneck
  • Overtime, fatigue, poor quality and higher accident risk

Over-hiring

  • Idle wages and congestion at the work front
  • More supervision; productivity drops
  • Idle workers leave, taking skills with them

Plan

  • Core crew of about 35 kept for the whole job (the M2–M4 level)
  • Flexible crew through the labour contractor with 2 weeks' notice from the 3-week look-ahead
  • Demobilise by trade as each phase finishes

Weekly review loop

Look-ahead (3 weeks)→Required crews→Muster vs plan→Output vs constants→Adjust hiring

H

Labour-supply contract checklist

Legal context. The four Labour Codes came into force on 21 Nov 2025, replacing earlier laws including the Contract Labour Act and the Minimum Wages Act. Contract-labour provisions of the OSH Code apply where 50 or more contract workers are engaged. Which registrations, licences and contributions apply depends on worker numbers, the establishment and the contractor's status. This checklist is for study, not legal advice; confirm under the current central and Karnataka rules.

Scope, pay and records

  • Scope by trade and number of workers; mobilisation and demobilisation notice
  • Wage rate per trade, payment cycle and mode (bank transfer)
  • Payment never below the applicable statutory minimum
  • Attendance / muster roll and wage register; wage slips
  • Identity and age verification; no child or forced labour
  • Deductions only as permitted, with records

Statutory and safety

  • Applicable registrations and licences of the contractor
  • PF / ESI applicability and monthly proof of compliance
  • Insurance and accident liability
  • Safety induction, PPE, toolbox talks, permits for work at height
  • Working hours, overtime, weekly rest and holidays
  • Accommodation, sanitation, drinking water and welfare responsibilities

Technical

  • Supervision and expected output tied to agreed productivity
  • Tools and equipment: who provides and maintains
  • Replacement of absent workers within an agreed time
  • Quality, rework and damage responsibility
  • Measurement and certification of work by the site engineer

Commercial and exit

  • Retention and release conditions
  • Dispute resolution and jurisdiction
  • Termination and notice
  • Records open to inspection by the principal employer
  • Principal employer's right to pay wages directly if the contractor defaults, and recover the amount

Data limitations & assumptions

  • No measured block-wise QTO: 12,000 m³ is allocated by floor area.
  • All labour constants, the formwork ratio, steel ratio, 55/45 sub/super split and the 24-month programme are assumptions.
  • Blockwork quantity is assumed (0.15 m³ per m²) and shown separately; finishes, MEP and excavation labour are excluded.
  • Market wage rates are planning assumptions; Zone 2 minimum-wage figures shown are basic rates only. Add current VDA and verify coverage and latest court status before payroll.
  • Statutory contribution, service charge and welfare percentages are placeholders.

Conclusion

From 12,000 m³ the model needs about 16,796 skilled and 14,873 unskilled man-days (31,669 in total) for the frame and blockwork. In the first programme the crew peaks at about 132 workers. Shifting the non-critical Phase 3 chain by its 2-month float lowers the peak to about 97 without moving the completion month. A core crew of about 35 plus a flexible contractor crew, paid at market rates that stay above the Zone 2 statutory minimums, gives a labour package of about Rs 4.32 crore under the stated assumptions.
