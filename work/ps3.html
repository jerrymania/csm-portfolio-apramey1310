B

Survey control strategy

Vertical control — benchmarks

A benchmark is a stable point of known reduced level (RL). Levels are carried from it with an auto level and staff. Permanent benchmarks (PBMs) stay for the whole project; temporary benchmarks (TBMs) are set near each work front for daily use.

Horizontal control — control stations and grid points

A control station is a point of known coordinates (X, Y) from which the total station sets out building grids. Grid reference points mark the ends of grid lines so lines can be re-established. These are not benchmarks, even if some also carry an RL.

Datum

Tie the site RLs to a reliable known benchmark (national or municipal) if one is reachable and its value is confirmed. If none is available, adopt an assumed datum (for example PBM-1 = RL 100.000 m) for internal use only, marked "assumed datum — not tied to national/municipal datum", approved in writing by the architect and used on every drawing.

Rules for siting control points

  • Outside the zone influenced by basement excavation, shoring, dewatering, stockpiles and truck movement. The safe distance depends on soil and excavation method — to be set with the geotechnical/shoring designer, not assumed.
  • Durable, protected, accessible, uniquely labelled and checkable from at least one other point.
  • Spread around the site so no single disturbance destroys the network; redundancy lets a disturbed point be detected.
  • Short level-transfer distances to every block and line of sight between neighbouring control stations.

Monuments within 5 days

A newly cast concrete pillar may still move in its first days. Preferred options: survey monuments installed with the consultant (for example a steel survey pin set firmly in concrete), and marks on existing stable structures only where the consultant verifies stability. RLs on new monuments are recorded as provisional at Visit 1 and confirmed against the reference at Visit 2. A survey bolt is not fixed to a boundary or compound wall unless its stability has been checked.

F

Why outsource when we have civil engineers?

Our engineers can do survey work. Outsourcing is a decision about equipment, accuracy, accountability and workload — not ability.

Calibrated equipment

Total station, prisms and data collector need regular calibration. Hiring a consultant for a few visits avoids buying and maintaining them.

Precision and specialist practice

Traverse adjustment, coordinate transformation and stake-out are daily work for a survey firm.

Independent verification

A third party fixing the datum gives an independent check of our own levels and lines.

Liability and traceability

The consultant signs for the control network, with records that can be audited if a level or boundary is disputed.

Workload peaks

Mobilisation is the busiest time for site engineers: excavation, shoring, receiving and approvals.

Speed

An experienced crew finishes the network and Phase 1 set-out in the time available.

Data limitations & assumptions

  • No reference benchmark value, soil data or grid drawing was supplied; PBM positions and the 6 control stations are a planning proposal.
  • Grid reference points per block are illustrative pending the grid drawings.
  • No RL or coordinate has been assumed; all are to be recorded during the survey.
  • Travel, stay, hire and engineer-day costs and the 7-visit alternative are assumptions; the saving range depends on them.
  • Closure tolerances are to be taken from the agreed survey specification.

Conclusion

Five permanent benchmarks (vertical control) and six control stations (horizontal control) are proposed outside the excavation zone, recorded in a register with RLs measured on site. All total-station work is bundled into three visits plus one contingency, costing about Rs 76,600–100,200 against about Rs 171,000 for phase-wise call-outs under our assumptions. Trained site engineers handle routine level transfer and monitoring. We outsource for calibrated equipment, independent verification, liability and workload — not because our engineers cannot survey.
