RERA is not a building permission

RERA registration protects buyers. It does not replace the building plan sanction, commencement permission or any NOC. A RERA application itself needs the sanctioned plan, so planning approval comes first.

Keep the layers separate

Title due diligence proves who owns the land. Building permission proves what may be built. Commencement permission allows work to start. NOCs cover specific risks. RERA governs sale. Each has its own owner and timing.

E

Concrete supply: RMC or own batching plant

Correction to our PS1 deck

Our deck compared RMC rates with nominal-mix site concrete for M25, M30 and M35 and reported a saving of about Rs 3.18 crore (32.5%). Nominal mixes are not valid mix designs for these grades — they need a design mix under IS 10262 — and the two sides were not priced on the same basis (transport, pumping, testing, plant, labour, wastage, overheads, taxes). That figure is withdrawn. The decision below rests on a weighted matrix, with an illustrative cost model that is clearly labelled as such.

Reading the model: under these assumptions an own plant only pays back above about 18,000 m³. At 12,000 m³ the cost gap is small (under 3%) — well inside the uncertainty of the inputs — so cost alone does not decide the question. Supply reliability, space and the investment risk do.

Recommendation and conveying

Adopt option A

RMC from two approved suppliers (one standby), each with an approved design mix per grade and a plant audit before the first pour.

Conditions to check first

  • Haul time from each plant to site at the hours we pour
  • Peak hourly demand for the largest raft pour vs plant output and truck fleet
  • Traffic and access through the material gate
  • Standby supplier for continuous pours

Conveying

  • Concrete pump with pipeline for rafts, basement walls and slabs
  • Boom placer or pump line for upper floors
  • Pump standing points planned at block edges reachable from the internal road
  • Crane and bucket as backup for small column pours

PS6 describes a later scenario in which the company sets up its own plant and uses local RMC meanwhile. That matches option C. If management chooses C, the RMC controls here still apply until the plant is commissioned.

Probable duration and working days

Method

  1. Work breakdownBy block and phase: excavation and shoring, basement raft and walls, basement slabs, superstructure, blockwork, finishes, MEP, external works.
  2. QuantitiesFrom the BOQ and drawings (concrete, steel, formwork, blockwork).
  3. DurationsQuantity ÷ (crew output × number of crews), checked against resource limits (PS5).
  4. Logic and critical pathDependencies between phases, curing and de-propping times, approvals; CPM to find the critical path.
  5. CalendarWorking days per week, public holidays, monsoon allowance, approval lead times.

Data limitations & assumptions

  • Block areas, 8 acres, 3 basements, 2 gates and ~12,000 m³ are from the assignment; whether block area is per floor or total is not stated.
  • Applicability of fire NOC, environmental clearance, airport height clearance and RERA depends on height, total built-up area including basements and the sale model — not given.

  • Welfare ratios must be checked against the OSH Code 2020 rules for Karnataka.
  • The duration is a range from an illustrative programme, not a CPM schedule.
K

Conclusion

We have mapped every approval to its owner, stage and condition, and separated title, building permission, NOCs and RERA. Ready-mix concrete from two approved suppliers scores highest (78.8 / 100) in the weighted matrix. The cost model shows only a small, assumption-dependent difference at 12,000 m³, so the earlier "Rs 3.18 crore saving" is withdrawn. The logistics plan keeps the seven blocks fixed and uses Gate 2 for materials and Gate 1 for people. The probable duration is 24–30 months (about 550–700 working days), to be confirmed by a CPM schedule.
